## What the model leaves out

- Federal only. No state tax (California has its own AMT), no NIIT, no payroll tax.
- Single filer, standard deduction, wages as the only other income, no other AMT preference items.
- Income, FMV, and tax parameters are held flat across the 4 schedule years. In practice the thresholds index each year and the FMV moves, so each year's crossover is different.
- Credit recovery assumes no sale. A later sale of the shares creates a negative AMT adjustment that can speed up recovery; a price drop can strand part of the credit.
- ISO expiration (10 years from grant, and usually 90 days after leaving) is not modeled.

## Two things worth knowing before you post

1. The crossover is small relative to the grant: about 320 options a year against 10,000. The video says so on screen (~1,300 of 10,000 in 4 years).
2. Splitting all 10,000 evenly over 4 years does not reduce AMT in this model: $589,903 total versus $567,476 all at once, because each year is far past the crossover and the exemption is fully phased out. That is why the video sizes years to the crossover instead of splitting evenly.
