    01ISO exercise plan and AMT

    Exercising and holding an ISO adds the spread to alternative minimum taxable income. The crossover is the largest exercise that still produces zero AMT. The starting plan exercises exactly that amount each year.

    02RSU withholding gap

    Employers withhold a flat supplemental rate on RSU income. The gap is the tax still owed at filing because the household's actual bracket is higher.

    03Sell-tranche plan after liquidity

    Each tranche sells a percentage of the shares that are sellable on that date. Vested NQSOs are exercised and sold the same day.

    Assumptions and method

    • Federal: 2026 ordinary brackets, standard deduction, long-term capital gain rates, 3.8% net investment income tax, and AMT (26%/28%, exemption with the 50% phase-out). Itemized deductions, payroll taxes (Social Security, Medicare, CA SDI), credits, the ISO $100,000 limit and prior-year AMT credits are not modeled.
    • California: 2026 rates are the 2025 FTB schedules indexed by the 3.4% California CPI change FTB announced in October 2026, with the announced 2026 standard deduction. FTB publishes the final 2026 schedules in late December; expect differences of a few dollars per bracket. CA AMT is 7% with a 25% exemption phase-out, indexed the same way. Capital gains are taxed as ordinary income; the 1% surcharge applies above $1,000,000 of taxable income.
    • ISO exercises are assumed on December 15 for the current year and January 15 for later years, lowest strike first. All AMT paid is treated as creditable in later years. A later sale of exercised ISO shares reverses the original spread for AMT, which is what releases the credit.
    • RSU withholding is 22% federal on the first $1,000,000 of supplemental wages in a year and 37% above it, plus 10.23% in California. Shares are assumed to be withheld to cover those rates; the net shares are held with basis equal to the vest price. RSUs that vested before this year use the grant FMV as basis.
    • Tax per tranche is the increase in that calendar year's total tax caused by adding the tranche, in date order. Future prices follow the scenario rate chosen at left; they are not forecasts. ESPP treatment is approximate (six-month offering period assumed).
    • Default grants for each company are invented, round-number examples. They are not any person's holdings. An uploaded vesting file is read inside this browser, is never transmitted or stored, and disappears on reset or reload. Uploaded grants use their actual vest dates and vest-date FMV; options count only shares still outstanding, so shares already exercised and held are not included.
