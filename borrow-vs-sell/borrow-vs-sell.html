1. The four options

Each cost sits next to its main risk. Borrowing against a portfolio can force sales at a loss if markets fall.

2. Tax savings: after-tax borrowing rates

Light bar = stated rate. Solid bar = rate after the tax effect modeled for these inputs. A tax saving that depends on an unsettled position (box spread) or on a deduction that may not apply is not assured.

Box spread: where the capital loss goes

Implied interest is modeled as a Section 1256 loss (60% long-term / 40% short-term, marked to market each year-end). It offsets capital gains first; without gains only per year offsets ordinary income and the rest carries forward. Confirm the tax treatment with the client's CPA.

3. Break-even: where borrowing beats selling, and where it loses

Net worth = portfolio minus loan balance, with every dollar of interest, payments, closing costs and tax savings carried at the portfolio return. Choose below whether the loan is repaid by selling stock at the end (tax paid then) or stays in place (the gain stays untaxed).

Above zero the borrowing path ends ahead of selling. Below zero it ends behind.
